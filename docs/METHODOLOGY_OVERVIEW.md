# AI WELLMAYBE — Methodology Overview

AI WELLMAYBE uses **AI Risk Ratings (AIRR)** as its underlying technical methodology/system for evidence-backed rating and diagnostic work.

The public methodology is deliberately understandable without publishing the internal mechanisms that would make gaming, evasion or replication easier.

## The question

The central question is not simply whether an AI system is “safe.”

It is:

> What is reasonably supported about this specific AI system, version or deployment, for this capability and scope, based on the evidence available now?

## Public principles

### Evidence over claims

Statements by owners, vendors, interfaces, documentation, benchmarks or third parties are inputs. They do not become conclusions merely because they are asserted.

### Identity, trust and permission are different

Knowing what a system is does not automatically establish what it can be trusted to do, what authority it has or whether a specific action should be permitted.

### Declared, observed and effective state may differ

A documented configuration, a visible interface and an effective runtime state are not automatically equivalent.

### Unknown remains visible

Insufficient evidence should remain identifiable as insufficient evidence. It should not silently become either a positive or a negative conclusion.

### Assessment depth is not a quality ladder

A deeper assessment can reveal a worse result. Assessment depth describes the depth and type of evidence obtained; it is not a medal, subscription tier or universal safety score.

### Confidence cannot outrun evidence

The strength of a conclusion is bounded by the quality, coverage, independence, relevance and freshness of the supporting evidence.

### History is versioned

Correction, remediation or reassessment may change the current state without pretending that earlier evidence or findings never existed.

### Payment does not buy a favorable result

Commercial participation, ownership claims, partnership, refusal to participate or institutional status are not themselves rating signals.

## What a public result should carry

Where applicable, a meaningful public result should preserve enough context to understand:

- which subject and rated version/configuration it concerns;
- what capability or scope was considered;
- what evidence boundary applied;
- what was assessed, not assessed or remained unknown;
- confidence and material limitations;
- freshness or material-change status;
- the methodology/version context needed to interpret the result.

## Deliberate non-disclosure

AI WELLMAYBE does not publish the full internal machinery behind rating integrity.

Hidden tests, exact thresholds, weights, exploit sequences, anti-gaming controls, internal adjudication routing and restricted evidence are intentionally outside this public overview.

Transparency should make a result interpretable. It should not make the evaluation system trivial to game.
